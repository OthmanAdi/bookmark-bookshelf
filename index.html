A reader that says what it is

Most read-later tools promise to pull the article out of any page. From a browser that promise breaks often: sites refuse the request, and doing it properly needs a server. This one makes a smaller promise and keeps it.

  • A link saved with a reading copy opens as a page and remembers your place, down to the paragraph.
  • A link saved alone says so. It offers a field to paste the text and a plain link to the original.
  • Nothing is fetched for you, nothing from a site is run, and nothing is sent anywhere.

Four parts, each usable alone

Shelf
Draws volumes for a pose and reports what was picked. No clock, no gestures, no storage.
Motion
Springs, drag, flick and snap on Pointer Events. It has no idea it is moving books.
Store
A versioned library in local storage with validation, migration, import and export.
Reader
Renders a saved copy as text nodes only, tracks the position and hands it back.

Smaller combinations that run on their own: shelf and motion, reader, store.

Built to be rebuilt

The repository carries a skill for coding agents. Point an agent at it and ask for the whole shelf or for one part: it gets the contracts, the constraints that keep the 3D row accessible, and the checks that prove the result.
